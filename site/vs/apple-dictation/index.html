FreeDictate vs Apple Dictation: Mac dictation vs Whisper

Apple Dictation is built in and free. So is FreeDictate. The difference is the speech model, and how you start and stop talking.

The short answer

Apple Dictation comes with every Mac, costs nothing and is a sensible place to start. FreeDictate is also free. It swaps Apple's speech recognizer for Whisper, the open speech model from OpenAI, running locally through whisper.cpp, and it gives you a push-to-talk button that works the same in every app.

If built-in Mac dictation works well for you, keep using it. If it mishears you, cuts off, or gets in the way while you type, Whisper is worth a try.

Accuracy: why Whisper

Whisper was trained on a very large and varied set of speech, and many people find it handles accents, fast speech, technical terms and punctuation well. FreeDictate uses large-v3-turbo, one of the stronger Whisper models that still runs quickly on a Mac. Results vary by voice, mic and vocabulary, so the honest advice is to try both on your own speech. The FreeDictate Setup window ends with a live "Try it" step for exactly that.

Live text vs finished text

Apple Dictation streams words onto the screen as you talk, which feels responsive and lets you see mistakes early. FreeDictate works on whole utterances: you talk, let go, and the full text is pasted at once. Whisper uses the context of everything you said to decide on words and punctuation, which is part of why it's accurate, and the result arrives a moment after you stop.

Push-to-talk

Built-in dictation uses a toggle shortcut. FreeDictate supports both styles. Hold your button and it stops the moment you let go, which is the fastest way to dictate a sentence. Tap to go hands-free, and tap again when you're done. Your button can be a lone modifier, a combo, or a mouse button.

How FreeDictate works

FreeDictate is a small open-source app built on Hammerspoon, ffmpeg and whisper.cpp. You pick one talk button: any key, a combo, a lone modifier like Right Option, or the middle, back or forward mouse button. Hold it and talk, then let go. Or tap it to start and tap again to stop. A press shorter than 0.35 seconds counts as a tap. Esc cancels.

While you talk, ffmpeg records 16 kHz mono audio to a temporary file and a small pill at the bottom of the screen shows a live level meter. When you stop, the audio goes to a local whisper-server on 127.0.0.1:8178, which only your Mac can reach. It runs the large-v3-turbo Whisper model, quantized to about 550 MB. If the server is down, FreeDictate falls back to whisper-cli. The text is pasted into whatever app has focus with ⌘V, and your clipboard is put back afterwards.

There is no account, no subscription and no pro tier. It's MIT-licensed, so you can read every line.

When to stick with Apple Dictation

  • It already works well for you. Built in beats installed.
  • You want to see words appear live as you speak.
  • You don't want to install anything, use Terminal or Homebrew.
  • You use voice commands or Voice Control features that are part of macOS.
  • Disk space is tight. FreeDictate's model is about 550 MB.

When FreeDictate is the better fit

  • Built-in dictation mishears you or keeps stopping.
  • You want hold-to-talk, or to use a mouse button.
  • You want a guarantee that speech never leaves your Mac.
  • You want the same behaviour in every app, pasted where your cursor is.

Try FreeDictate in a few minutes

You need a Mac and Homebrew. One command installs whisper-cpp, ffmpeg and Hammerspoon, downloads the speech model once, and opens a Setup window that walks you through microphone access, Accessibility access and choosing your button, then lets you try it live.

git clone https://github.com/owenmonagan/freedictate.git
cd freedictate
./install.sh

If it isn't for you, ./uninstall.sh removes it. See the install guide for details.
