FreeDictate vs Wispr Flow: the free, offline alternative

Wispr Flow is a polished, AI-assisted dictation service. FreeDictate does one thing: hold a button, talk, and your words are pasted. Free, and nothing leaves your Mac.

The short answer

If you want a free Wispr Flow alternative that runs entirely on your Mac, FreeDictate is built for exactly that. It costs nothing, needs no account, and transcribes with whisper.cpp on-device. If you want AI rewriting, a cross-device service and a team plan, Wispr Flow offers more of that, and it charges for it.

Both tools share the same core idea: dictate into any app, not just a single text box. The difference is what sits around that core. Wispr Flow is a commercial product with a subscription. FreeDictate is one button and an MIT license.

Is it Wispr Flow or Whisper Flow?

The product is spelled Wispr Flow, but many people search for it as "Whisper Flow". It is the same app. It is a different thing from OpenAI's Whisper speech model. FreeDictate does use Whisper, through whisper.cpp running on your Mac, so if you were looking for a Whisper-powered Flow-style dictation app that is free and offline, this is it.

Privacy: on-device vs cloud

Wispr Flow sends your audio to remote servers for transcription and AI cleanup. That is how it can do heavier processing, and the company publishes its own privacy policy describing how it handles data. Whether that trade is fine depends on what you dictate.

FreeDictate never sends audio anywhere. The speech engine listens only on 127.0.0.1, so nothing outside your Mac can reach it. Recording is a temporary local file. There is no telemetry and no login, because there is no server for us to log you into. If you dictate client notes, medical details or code under NDA, that difference matters.

Price: subscription vs free

Wispr Flow is a paid subscription for regular use. FreeDictate is free, and not in the trial sense. There is no word limit, no pro tier and nothing to upgrade to. The only cost is about 550 MB of disk for the speech model, downloaded once.

How FreeDictate works

FreeDictate is a small open-source app built on Hammerspoon, ffmpeg and whisper.cpp. You pick one talk button: any key, a combo, a lone modifier like Right Option, or the middle, back or forward mouse button. Hold it and talk, then let go. Or tap it to start and tap again to stop. A press shorter than 0.35 seconds counts as a tap. Esc cancels.

While you talk, ffmpeg records 16 kHz mono audio to a temporary file and a small pill at the bottom of the screen shows a live level meter. When you stop, the audio goes to a local whisper-server on 127.0.0.1:8178, which only your Mac can reach. It runs the large-v3-turbo Whisper model, quantized to about 550 MB. If the server is down, FreeDictate falls back to whisper-cli. The text is pasted into whatever app has focus with ⌘V, and your clipboard is put back afterwards.

There is no account, no subscription and no pro tier. It's MIT-licensed, so you can read every line.

Setup: configure less

Feature-rich dictation apps come with modes, styles, dictionaries and settings to tune. That's useful for some people and noise for others. FreeDictate has one setting that matters: your button. Install, choose your button, talk. A Setup window handles permissions and lets you test it live before you rely on it.

When to choose Wispr Flow instead

  • You want AI to rewrite as you talk. If you want filler words removed, tone adjusted, or rough speech turned into a tidy email, Wispr Flow is designed for that. FreeDictate pastes what Whisper hears.
  • You dictate on more than a Mac. FreeDictate is macOS only. Wispr Flow is available on other platforms.
  • You need team features or support. A commercial product can offer billing, admin controls and a support desk. FreeDictate is a GitHub project.
  • Your Mac is older or slow. On-device transcription uses your own CPU and GPU. Cloud processing doesn't. Apple silicon handles FreeDictate well; an older Intel Mac will be slower.

When FreeDictate is the better fit

  • You don't want another subscription.
  • You want dictation to work on a plane, or anywhere without Wi-Fi.
  • You want your audio to stay on your Mac.
  • You'd like to talk with a mouse button, not just a keyboard shortcut.
  • You want to read the code that's listening to your microphone.

Try FreeDictate in a few minutes

You need a Mac and Homebrew. One command installs whisper-cpp, ffmpeg and Hammerspoon, downloads the speech model once, and opens a Setup window that walks you through microphone access, Accessibility access and choosing your button, then lets you try it live.

git clone https://github.com/owenmonagan/freedictate.git
cd freedictate
./install.sh

If it isn't for you, ./uninstall.sh removes it. See the install guide for details.
