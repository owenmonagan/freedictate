FreeDictate vs MacWhisper: a free MacWhisper alternative for dictation

MacWhisper shines at transcribing recordings. FreeDictate is for live dictation: hold a button, talk, and the text lands in the app you're using.

The short answer

MacWhisper and FreeDictate both run Whisper on your Mac, but they're built for different jobs. MacWhisper is best known for transcribing audio and video files: interviews, meetings, podcasts. It has a free version and a paid upgrade. FreeDictate is for dictation: you hold a button, talk, and your words are pasted where your cursor is. It's free, with no paid tier.

If you came looking for a MacWhisper alternative because you want to talk instead of type, FreeDictate is focused on exactly that. If you need to transcribe a recording, MacWhisper is the better tool.

Dictation vs transcription

These sound similar but feel very different in daily use. Transcription is about a finished recording: you drop in a file, wait, and get a transcript with timestamps you can edit and export. Dictation is about the next sentence you're about to write: it should start the instant you press and finish a moment after you stop.

FreeDictate is tuned for the second case. A local whisper-server keeps the model loaded, so there's no startup delay on each press. Audio is recorded at 16 kHz mono, the format Whisper expects, and the result is pasted into whatever app has focus, with your clipboard restored afterwards.

How FreeDictate works

FreeDictate is a small open-source app built on Hammerspoon, ffmpeg and whisper.cpp. You pick one talk button: any key, a combo, a lone modifier like Right Option, or the middle, back or forward mouse button. Hold it and talk, then let go. Or tap it to start and tap again to stop. A press shorter than 0.35 seconds counts as a tap. Esc cancels.

While you talk, ffmpeg records 16 kHz mono audio to a temporary file and a small pill at the bottom of the screen shows a live level meter. When you stop, the audio goes to a local whisper-server on 127.0.0.1:8178, which only your Mac can reach. It runs the large-v3-turbo Whisper model, quantized to about 550 MB. If the server is down, FreeDictate falls back to whisper-cli. The text is pasted into whatever app has focus with ⌘V, and your clipboard is put back afterwards.

There is no account, no subscription and no pro tier. It's MIT-licensed, so you can read every line.

Privacy

Both tools can run Whisper locally, which is a big part of their appeal. FreeDictate has no cloud option at all, so there's nothing to check or switch off. The speech engine listens only on 127.0.0.1.

When to choose MacWhisper instead

  • You transcribe recordings. Meetings, interviews, lectures and podcasts are MacWhisper's home turf. FreeDictate doesn't import files.
  • You need timestamps, subtitles or export formats. FreeDictate just pastes plain text.
  • You want to choose models or compare their output.
  • You want a native app with a full editing interface.

When FreeDictate is the better fit

  • You want to speak instead of type, in any app, all day.
  • You want a push-to-talk button, including a mouse button.
  • You want it free and open source.

Plenty of people use both: MacWhisper for recordings, FreeDictate for writing.

Try FreeDictate in a few minutes

You need a Mac and Homebrew. One command installs whisper-cpp, ffmpeg and Hammerspoon, downloads the speech model once, and opens a Setup window that walks you through microphone access, Accessibility access and choosing your button, then lets you try it live.

git clone https://github.com/owenmonagan/freedictate.git
cd freedictate
./install.sh

If it isn't for you, ./uninstall.sh removes it. See the install guide for details.
