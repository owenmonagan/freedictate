Free Wispr Flow alternatives for Mac

Wispr Flow is good at what it does. If you want something free, offline, or simpler, here are the options worth knowing, including ours.

Why people look for a Wispr Flow alternative

Wispr Flow is a polished, AI-assisted dictation service that works across apps. People usually look elsewhere for one of three reasons: they don't want another subscription, they'd rather their audio stayed on their own Mac, or they want something with fewer settings. Each tool below solves some of those. We make the first one, so we've put it first, but we've tried to be fair to the rest.

1. FreeDictate

Free, offline, one button. FreeDictate runs Whisper locally through whisper.cpp. You pick a talk button (any key, combo, lone modifier, or the middle, back or forward mouse button), hold it and talk, then let go. Your words are pasted into whatever app has focus and your clipboard is restored. Tap instead of holding to go hands-free. Esc cancels.

There's no account, no subscription and no pro tier. It's MIT-licensed. It installs with one command using Homebrew, which adds whisper-cpp, ffmpeg and Hammerspoon and downloads a roughly 550 MB speech model once.

Trade-offs: Mac only, no AI rewriting, no modes, and installing means pasting a line into Terminal.

2. Apple Dictation

Free and already on your Mac. Turn it on in System Settings under Keyboard and press the shortcut to talk. Words appear live as you speak, and on supported Macs many languages are processed on-device. It's the obvious first thing to try.

Trade-offs: it uses Apple's own recognizer rather than Whisper, uses a toggle shortcut instead of hold-to-talk, and some people find it less accurate for their voice or vocabulary.

3. VoiceInk

Open-source, on-device, native. VoiceInk is a native Mac app that runs Whisper locally, with model choice and optional enhancements. Its source is public, and it sells a license for the ready-to-run app.

Trade-offs: not free to use out of the box, and more settings than some people want.

4. Superwhisper

Feature-rich and flexible. Superwhisper offers local Whisper models plus modes and optional AI processing, so you can set up different behaviour for different kinds of writing. It's a paid product with a limited free option.

Trade-offs: it costs money for full use, and the flexibility comes with choices to make.

5. MacWhisper

Best for recordings. MacWhisper is mainly a file transcription app: drop in an interview or meeting and get a transcript, on-device. It has a free version and a paid upgrade.

Trade-offs: live push-to-talk dictation into any app is not its main focus.

When Wispr Flow is still the right choice

If you want your speech cleaned up and rewritten by AI as you talk, want the same tool on more than one platform, or need team billing and support, Wispr Flow is built for that and the alternatives above mostly aren't. Paying for something that fits is a good trade.

How to choose

  • Want it free and private, with nothing to configure? FreeDictate.
  • Don't want to install anything? Apple Dictation.
  • Want a native app with options, and happy to pay? VoiceInk or Superwhisper.
  • Transcribing files rather than dictating? MacWhisper.

Details here are as of Nov 2026 and stated broadly. Pricing and features change, so check each product's site before deciding.

Try FreeDictate in a few minutes

You need a Mac and Homebrew. One command installs whisper-cpp, ffmpeg and Hammerspoon, downloads the speech model once, and opens a Setup window that walks you through microphone access, Accessibility access and choosing your button, then lets you try it live.

git clone https://github.com/owenmonagan/freedictate.git
cd freedictate
./install.sh

If it isn't for you, ./uninstall.sh removes it. See the install guide for details.
