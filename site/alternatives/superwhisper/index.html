Superwhisper alternatives: free options for Mac

Superwhisper is a strong, flexible Whisper app. If you want something free, open source, or simpler, these are the alternatives worth a look.

Why look for a Superwhisper alternative

Superwhisper brings Whisper to the Mac with modes, a choice of models and optional AI processing. It's well made. People usually look for an alternative because they want something free, something open source they can inspect, or something with far fewer decisions. Here are the honest options. FreeDictate is ours, so it's first, and we've tried to describe the others fairly.

1. FreeDictate

The free, zero-configuration option. FreeDictate uses the same Whisper family of models, running locally through whisper.cpp. It picks one model for you, large-v3-turbo at about 550 MB, and keeps a local whisper-server running on 127.0.0.1:8178 so there's no load time when you press your button.

You choose one talk button: any key, combo, lone modifier, or the middle, back or forward mouse button. Hold and talk, or tap to start and tap again to stop. The text is pasted into the focused app and your clipboard is restored. It's MIT-licensed, with no account and no pro tier.

Trade-offs: no modes, no AI post-processing, no model picker, Mac only, and it installs from Terminal with Homebrew.

2. VoiceInk

The closest open-source match. VoiceInk is a native Mac app that runs Whisper on-device and offers model choice and optional enhancements, which makes it closer to Superwhisper in feel. Its code is public, and it sells a license for the packaged app.

Trade-offs: it isn't free to use out of the box, and it has more to configure than FreeDictate.

3. Apple Dictation

The built-in option. Every Mac has it. Turn it on in System Settings, press the shortcut, and words appear live. Many languages run on-device on supported Macs.

Trade-offs: it isn't Whisper, it uses a toggle rather than hold-to-talk, and accuracy for technical vocabulary varies.

4. MacWhisper

For recordings. If part of why you use Superwhisper is transcribing files, MacWhisper is a strong on-device choice, with a free version and a paid upgrade.

Trade-offs: live dictation into any app isn't its main focus.

5. Wispr Flow

For AI rewriting. If the AI side of Superwhisper is what you value most, Wispr Flow focuses heavily on cleaning up and formatting speech. It processes in the cloud and is a subscription.

Trade-offs: not on-device, and not free for regular use.

When to stay with Superwhisper

If you rely on modes for different kinds of writing, switch models often, or want AI to reformat your words, Superwhisper does that well and most of the alternatives here don't. That's a good reason to keep paying for it.

How to choose

  • Free, private, nothing to set up: FreeDictate.
  • Open source with more knobs: VoiceInk.
  • No install at all: Apple Dictation.
  • Mostly transcribing files: MacWhisper.
  • Mostly want AI cleanup: Wispr Flow.

Details are as of Nov 2026 and kept deliberately broad. Check each product's site for current pricing and features.

Try FreeDictate in a few minutes

You need a Mac and Homebrew. One command installs whisper-cpp, ffmpeg and Hammerspoon, downloads the speech model once, and opens a Setup window that walks you through microphone access, Accessibility access and choosing your button, then lets you try it live.

git clone https://github.com/owenmonagan/freedictate.git
cd freedictate
./install.sh

If it isn't for you, ./uninstall.sh removes it. See the install guide for details.
