Privacy · Offline · Open source

Nothing leaves your Mac

FreeDictate has no account, no cloud and no analytics. Your voice is turned into text on your Mac, and you can read the code that does it.

The short version

  • Your audio is recorded and transcribed on your Mac. It is never uploaded.
  • FreeDictate has no account, no sign-in and no server of its own.
  • The app sends no analytics, telemetry or crash reports.
  • This website has no analytics or tracking scripts either.
  • All of it is open source, so you don't have to take our word for it.

What FreeDictate connects to

While you dictate, FreeDictate makes exactly one kind of network request: it sends your recording to the speech engine at http://127.0.0.1:8178. That address is your own Mac. The speech engine, whisper-server, is set to listen only on 127.0.0.1, so other devices on your network can't reach it. FreeDictate also checks that same local address to see whether the engine is running. If it isn't, FreeDictate runs whisper-cli directly on your Mac, which makes no network connection at all.

The app contains no other web addresses it talks to. You can confirm this yourself: open app/dictate.lua and search for http. The only addresses are the two local ones above.

The one time it goes online.

The installer downloads software with Homebrew and fetches the speech model once from the whisper.cpp model repository. After that, dictation works with Wi-Fi off.

What is stored on your Mac

WhatWhereWhy
Your latest recording$TMPDIR/freedictate.wavThe audio being transcribed. It is replaced by each new recording, and lives in your private temporary folder.
The speech model~/.local/share/whisper/ggml-large-v3-turbo-q5_0.binThe whisper.cpp model, about 550 MB. It contains no data about you.
Your settingsHammerspoon's settingsYour talk key and whether Setup has run.
Speech engine log~/Library/Logs/freedictate-whisper-server.logStart-up and error messages from whisper-server.

FreeDictate keeps no history of what you've said. Transcripts are pasted and not saved.

Your clipboard

To type your words, FreeDictate briefly puts the transcript on your clipboard, presses ⌘V, and then puts back whatever you had copied before. Your clipboard ends up as you left it. The one exception: if Hammerspoon doesn't have Accessibility access, FreeDictate can't paste, so it leaves the transcript on the clipboard for you to paste yourself.

Permissions

Hammerspoon, which runs FreeDictate, asks for two macOS permissions:

  • Microphone, used only while you are dictating or testing your mic in Setup.
  • Accessibility, used to notice your talk key and to paste.

You can turn either off at any time in System Settings, under Privacy & Security.

This website

This site is plain HTML and CSS on GitHub Pages. It loads no analytics, no ad or tracking scripts, no cookies and no third-party fonts. GitHub, which hosts it, may keep standard server logs, as any web host does.

Check it yourself

FreeDictate is MIT-licensed and the full source is on GitHub. The app is one Lua file, the installer is one shell script, and the speech engine setup is one LaunchAgent template, launchd/dev.freedictate.whisper-server.plist.template, where you can see --host 127.0.0.1 for yourself. To go further, turn off Wi-Fi and dictate. It works the same.
