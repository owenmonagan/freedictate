Voice to text on Mac

Every way to turn your voice into text on a Mac, from the dictation that comes free with macOS to paid and open-source apps. What each one does well, and how to choose.

Voice to text, also called speech to text or dictation, means you talk and your Mac types what you said. The useful kind works wherever your cursor is: an email, a chat message, a document, a prompt box. This guide covers the options on Mac as of November 2026, including two that cost nothing.

The options

Apple Dictation

Built into macOS and free. Turn it on in System Settings under Keyboard, then press the dictation shortcut (by default, press fn twice, or the microphone key on newer keyboards) and talk. It works in most text fields. Whether audio is processed on your Mac or sent to Apple depends on your macOS version, your Mac and the language, so check Apple's settings for your setup. It's the easiest place to start. See FreeDictate vs Apple Dictation.

Dictation built into a single tool

Some apps have their own voice input, such as an AI coding assistant like Claude Code or a code editor's voice feature. These can be good, but they only work inside that one tool. If you want to dictate into Mail, Slack and your terminal too, you need something system-wide. See voice to text for coding.

Paid dictation apps

Apps like Wispr Flow and Superwhisper are polished and add features on top of transcription: modes, styles, AI rewriting and settings. They are sold as a subscription or license, and some process audio in the cloud. Pricing and features change, so check their sites. See vs Wispr Flow and vs Superwhisper.

Open-source Whisper apps

Whisper is an open speech model that many apps build on. VoiceInk is an open-source dictation app built on it. MacWhisper is aimed mainly at transcribing audio and video files rather than live dictation. See vs VoiceInk and vs MacWhisper.

FreeDictate

Free and open source, MIT licensed. You pick one button, any key, combo or mouse button, hold it and talk, and your words are pasted into whatever app has focus. Speech is transcribed on your Mac by whisper.cpp with the Whisper large-v3 turbo model, so it works offline and your voice never leaves your computer. There's no account and no paid tier. See how it works.

How accurate is FreeDictate?

We tested it on 5 November 2026 on an Apple M5 MacBook Pro, using whisper-server with the ggml-large-v3-turbo-q5_0 model. The test was 40 clips: 10 dictation-style sentences, each read by four macOS voices (Samantha, Daniel, Karen and Moira).

  • Word error rate: 1.8%.
  • Speed: a median of 0.64 seconds from audio sent to text returned. The slowest clip took 0.84 seconds.
  • The misses: “setup” came back as “set up”, “diff” as “div”, and “middleware” as “middle way”.

Synthetic voices are cleaner than a real person talking into a laptop mic, so expect real-world accuracy to be lower. We didn't test Apple Dictation or other apps, so this isn't a comparison. Anyone can rerun it with tools/bench.py in the repo.

How to choose

  • You dictate now and then: start with Apple Dictation. It's already on your Mac.
  • You want the same button and results in every app: use a system-wide app. FreeDictate is free; paid apps add extra features.
  • Your voice must stay on your Mac: pick an on-device option and check how it handles audio. FreeDictate runs fully offline after setup. See offline speech to text on Mac.
  • You want AI rewriting and modes: a paid app is built for that. FreeDictate deliberately does one thing.
  • You want to use a mouse button: see mouse button dictation on Mac.

Questions

What is the best free voice to text for Mac?

There are two free options that work across apps. Apple Dictation is built in and quick to turn on. FreeDictate is free and open source, runs Whisper large-v3 turbo on your Mac, and starts with one button you choose. Try both and keep the one that suits how you work.

Is there free speech to text for Mac that works offline?

Yes. FreeDictate runs whisper.cpp on your Mac and needs the internet only to install and download the model once. Apple Dictation can also process some languages on-device on recent Macs, depending on your macOS version and settings.

Why not use the voice input built into my coding tool?

It works well inside that tool, but only there. If you also want to dictate into Mail, Slack, your editor and your terminal, a system-wide app like FreeDictate gives you the same button everywhere.
