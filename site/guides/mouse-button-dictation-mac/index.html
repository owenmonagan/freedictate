Mouse button dictation on Mac

Your mouse already has a spare button. Make it your talk button: hold it, say what you mean, let go, and the words land wherever your cursor is.

Most dictation on a Mac starts from the keyboard. That makes sense if your hands are already on it. But a lot of the day your right hand is on the mouse: reading, scrolling, clicking into a reply box. Reaching back to the keyboard to start dictation breaks the flow. Mouse button dictation fixes that. You press a button you are already touching, talk, and let go.

FreeDictate supports this out of the box. Your talk button can be any key or combo, or the middle, back or forward mouse button. It's free, it runs offline, and there is nothing else to set up.

Why a mouse button works so well

  • It's already under your finger. Click into a text field, then hold the side button and talk. No hand movement at all.
  • It's usually unused. Back and forward buttons mostly do browser navigation. Many people never press them. The middle button is often a spare too.
  • Holding feels natural. Press to talk, release to stop is the same gesture as a walkie-talkie. You don't have to remember whether you're recording.

Set it up in four steps

  1. Install FreeDictate. From Terminal: 
    git clone https://github.com/owenmonagan/freedictate.git
cd freedictate
./install.sh
    The installer sets up Hammerspoon, ffmpeg, whisper.cpp and the speech model with Homebrew. See the install guide for details.
  2. Grant permissions. The Setup window asks for Microphone access and Accessibility access for Hammerspoon. Accessibility is what lets FreeDictate paste into other apps.
  3. Choose your button. On the "Your button" step, press the mouse button you want. Later you can change it from the FreeDictate menu bar icon with Set talk key…. When it asks you to press something, click the middle, back or forward button.
  4. Try it. The last step of Setup is a live test. Hold the button, say a sentence, and let go.

Left and right click are left alone.

FreeDictate listens for the extra mouse buttons only: middle, back and forward. Your normal clicks keep working exactly as before.

Using it day to day

Do thisWhat happens
Hold your button, talk, let goRecording stops when you release. Your words are pasted.
Tap your button, talk, tap againHands-free. A press shorter than 0.35 seconds counts as a tap.
Press Esc while listeningCancels. Nothing is pasted.

While you talk, a small pill at the bottom of the screen shows a live level meter, so you can see the microphone hears you. When you stop, it shows that it's transcribing, then pastes the text into the app that has focus with ⌘V. Your clipboard is put back the way it was afterwards.

Hold is best for short bursts: a chat reply, a search, a commit message. Tap is better for longer passages, since you don't have to keep the button pressed. Tap once, talk as long as you need, tap again.

Which button to pick

Back or forward (side buttons)

The thumb buttons on most mice are the easiest to hold. Choose the one you use less for browsing. While it's your talk button, FreeDictate takes that press, so it won't also go back a page.

Middle button

Good if your mouse has a clickable wheel and no side buttons. Holding a wheel click for a long sentence can be tiring, so the tap mode is handy here.

Trackpad only?

Trackpads don't have extra buttons. Use a keyboard key instead. A lone modifier like Right Option works well, since you rarely press it on its own. The push to talk guide covers keyboard choices.

Mouse utilities and button remapping

Some mice come with their own software that remaps the extra buttons to shortcuts. If a vendor app turns your back button into something else, FreeDictate may never see a mouse button press. If your button doesn't register, turn off the remapping for that button in the vendor app, then run Set talk key… again. Alternatively, have the vendor app send a key combo, and set that combo as your talk key.

Private by default

Everything happens on your Mac. FreeDictate records 16 kHz mono audio to a temporary file only while you're talking, and sends it to a local whisper.cpp server on 127.0.0.1, which only your Mac can reach. No account, no cloud, no subscription.

If something's off

  • Nothing happens when you press the button. Check that Hammerspoon is running and the FreeDictate icon is in the menu bar, then set the talk key again.
  • Text is copied but not pasted. Hammerspoon needs Accessibility access. Your words are on the clipboard in the meantime.
  • Want to go back to the keyboard? Choose Reset to ⌥Space from the menu.
