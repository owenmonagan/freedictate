Voice typing for RSI on Mac

If typing hurts, every sentence you can say instead of type is one less to type. Here's how to set up dictation that asks as little of your hands as possible.

This isn't medical advice.

Pain, numbness or tingling in your hands, wrists or arms is worth taking seriously. Please see a doctor or physical therapist. Dictation is a tool for typing less. It isn't a treatment.

Repetitive strain injury, or RSI, is a common name for pain from repeated movements like typing and using a mouse. Many people who deal with it look for ways to cut down on keystrokes. Voice typing is one of the most direct: you say the sentence and it appears.

FreeDictate is a free dictation app for Mac designed around one button. That matters here, because the fewer and easier the presses, the better.

Why one button helps

Some dictation setups need several steps: open a window, pick a mode, start, stop, copy, paste. Each step is more pressing and clicking. FreeDictate is one press to start and one to stop, or one hold. The text is pasted for you into the app you're using, so you don't need to copy and paste it yourself.

Pick the button that's easiest for you

Your talk button can be any key, any combo, a lone modifier, or the middle, back or forward mouse button. Choose based on what's comfortable, not on what's conventional.

  • A single key instead of a combo. Combos need two fingers at once. A lone modifier like Right Option, or an unused function key, needs one.
  • A mouse side button if your hand rests on the mouse and the keyboard is the problem. See mouse button dictation.
  • Something big and easy to find without looking, so you're not hunting for it.

Change it any time from the FreeDictate menu bar icon with Set talk key….

Tap instead of hold

Holding a key for a whole paragraph is its own strain. FreeDictate lets you tap instead:

Do thisWhat happens
Hold your button, talk, let goRecording stops when you release. Your words are pasted.
Tap your button, talk, tap againHands-free. A press shorter than 0.35 seconds counts as a tap.
Press Esc while listeningCancels. Nothing is pasted.

For anything longer than a sentence or two, tap mode is usually more comfortable. Tap once, rest your hands, talk, and tap again when you're done.

Set it up

  1. Install from Terminal. If typing is hard, you can paste this line instead of typing it: 
    git clone https://github.com/owenmonagan/freedictate.git
cd freedictate
./install.sh
  2. In the Setup window, allow Microphone and Accessibility access for Hammerspoon.
  3. On the "Your button" step, press the button that's easiest for you.
  4. Use the live test at the end to make sure it feels right. If it doesn't, pick a different button.

FreeDictate starts at login, so your button is ready after every restart without anything to click.

Getting more out of voice typing

  • Dictate the draft, edit lightly. Say the whole message in one go, then fix a word or two by hand. Much less typing than writing from scratch.
  • Speak in sentences. Whisper adds punctuation from natural speech, so you rarely need to type commas and full stops.
  • Use it everywhere. It pastes into any app with focus: email, chat, documents, browser forms, notes.
  • Cancel freely. Press Esc while listening and nothing is pasted. No cleanup needed.

Other things that may help

Dictation works well alongside other changes. People with RSI often look at their desk and chair setup, take regular breaks, and use macOS accessibility features such as Keyboard shortcuts, Sticky Keys or Voice Control, which can control the Mac by voice. A professional can help you decide what's right for you.

Free and private

FreeDictate costs nothing and has no subscription, so it's easy to try without committing. It runs entirely on your Mac with whisper.cpp, so what you say, including anything personal, stays on your computer.
